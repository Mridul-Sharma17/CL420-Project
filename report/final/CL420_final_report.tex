\subsection*{Abstract}
A fed batch process for extracellular recombinant \rol{} production by \pp{} is evaluated using Monod and substrate inhibition kinetics. Published rate functions and parameters are implemented in Python. Four material balances include nutrient feed volume and explicit methanol feeding policies. Eight reference simulations are followed by a constant substrate setpoint screen, oxygen transfer calculations, and a batch cost estimate. Under the inhibition model, a screened methanol setpoint of \Setpoint{} g L\textsuperscript{\(-1\)} gives a predicted harvest activity of \FinalActivity{} U mL\textsuperscript{\(-1\)} after \FinalTime{} h of induction. The corresponding productivity is \FinalProductivity{} U L\textsuperscript{\(-1\)} h\textsuperscript{\(-1\)}. Oxygen enrichment and an independently qualified transfer capacity are necessary for this candidate operating point. The study connects kinetic model selection to feeding, oxygen supply, product recovery, and operating cost. All performance results are model predictions.
\par\endgroup

\newpage
\section{Product and process definition}
\subsection{Product, application and design basis}
Recombinant \rol{} catalyses lipid hydrolysis and can support ester synthesis and transesterification. Its industrial applications include biodiesel and structured lipid production. The proposed product is a concentrated liquid enzyme preparation for industrial biocatalysis. The target customers are enzyme formulators and process developers \cite{PonteThesis,Lopez2019}.

The design is based on a nominal 5 L stirred vessel and a 2 L initial induction volume. A 3.5 L working limit provides headspace for aeration and foam. Product output is expressed as lipase activity, with concentration in U mL$^{-1}$ and total batch activity in U. Production and costs are evaluated per batch and per million recovered U.

\subsection{Host and metabolic basis}
The host is \pp{} X-33 carrying pPICZ$\alpha$ROL under the methanol induced $P_{AOX1}$ promoter, with the Mut\textsuperscript{+} phenotype. This strain secretes ROL into the broth, which allows cell removal without cell disruption. The host and cultivation conditions are adopted from the published ROL process \cite{PonteThesis}.

Glycerol first supports biomass growth. After a transition phase, methanol acts as both carbon source and inducer. Alcohol oxidase converts methanol to formaldehyde and hydrogen peroxide. Catalase removes hydrogen peroxide. Formaldehyde is either assimilated through the xylulose monophosphate pathway or oxidized towards carbon dioxide for energy generation. Oxygen is required for methanol oxidation and respiration. Carbon dioxide and metabolic heat are important process outputs \cite{PonteThesis}.

\section{Process configuration and operating basis}
Fed batch operation controls residual methanol while avoiding continuous product withdrawal. It also separates biomass development from induction. Excess methanol can inhibit growth and production, while insufficient methanol limits the induced process. This supports controlled feeding rather than a large initial methanol charge \cite{Ponte2018}.

The proposed 0.13 m tank diameter gives a 151 mm liquid depth at 2 L. Impeller centres at 40 and 105 mm, with 13 mm blades, keep both turbines submerged initially.

\subsection{Inoculum, medium and process sequence}
The proposed inoculum stage adopts YPD medium in baffled flasks for 24 h at 30\,$^{\circ}$C and 150 rpm. Cells are collected and resuspended in basal medium. The seed stage can run in parallel with reactor preparation \cite{PonteThesis}.

The basal medium contains, per litre, 40 g glycerol, 26.7 mL of 85\% phosphoric acid, 0.93 g CaSO$_4$, 18.2 g K$_2$SO$_4$, 14.9 g MgSO$_4\!\cdot\!$7H$_2$O, 4.13 g KOH, 5 mL trace stock, 2 mL biotin stock and 0.5 mL antifoam. Trace stock supplies Fe, Zn, Cu, Mn, Co and other micronutrients \cite{PonteThesis}.

After glycerol depletion, the design adopts a 5 h transition with glycerol feeding and stepwise methanol addition. Induction then uses methanol with trace nutrients and a linked 200 g L$^{-1}$ NH$_4$Cl feed. The ratio is 0.12 g NH$_4$Cl per g methanol \cite{PonteThesis}. The simulation begins at induction, not at inoculation. The scheduling allowance is 18 h for the glycerol batch, 5 h for transition, \FinalTime{} h for induction and 6 h for turnaround. The transition duration is literature based. The resulting reactor occupancy is \Occupancy{} h.

\newpage
\section{Growth and product formation kinetics}
The inhibition model uses substrate dependent growth and production rates. The Monod comparator removes the inhibition term while retaining the same nominal coefficients \cite{PonteThesis}.
\begin{align}
q_i^{\mathrm{H}}(S)&=q_{\max,i}\frac{S}{K_{S,i}+S+S^2/K_{I,i}},\\
q_i^{\mathrm{M}}(S)&=q_{\max,i}\frac{S}{K_{S,i}+S},\qquad i=X,P.
\end{align}
Here $q_X=\mu$. This comparison tests the effect of a modelling assumption. It is not a separate fit of Monod parameters to experimental data.

\begin{center}\tighttable
\captionof{table}{Kinetic inputs from the thesis, Table 5.1 \cite{PonteThesis}.}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}\toprule
Parameter & Value & Unit\\\midrule
$\mu_{\max},\ K_{S,X},\ K_{I,X}$ & 0.069, 0.50, 7.5 & h$^{-1}$, g L$^{-1}$, g L$^{-1}$\\
$q_{\max,P},\ K_{S,P},\ K_{I,P}$ & 2200, 10.0, 1.2 & U g$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$, g L$^{-1}$, g L$^{-1}$\\
$Y_{S/X},\ m_S$ & 5.14, 0.013 & g g$^{-1}$, g g$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$\\
$Y_{O_2/X},\ m_{O_2}$ & 0.206, $6.1\times10^{-4}$ & mol g$^{-1}$, mol g$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{equation}
q_S=5.14\mu+0.013,\qquad q_{O_2}=0.206\mu+0.00061.
\end{equation}
The units are g methanol g$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$ and mol O$_2$ g$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$, respectively. The coefficient 5.14 is substrate per biomass. Its reciprocal, 0.1946 g g$^{-1}$, is the intrinsic biomass yield before maintenance.

\fig{kinetic_models}{0.98}{Rate laws used in the study. Inhibition produces finite substrate optima.}

The inhibition peaks occur at $S=\sqrt{K_SK_I}$, about 1.94 g L$^{-1}$ for growth and 3.46 g L$^{-1}$ for production. The achieved peak rates are 0.0455 h$^{-1}$ and 324.8 U g$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$, below the nominal coefficients. Production is modelled as growth linked during methanol induction, with a separate $q_P(S)$ law and a variable activity to biomass yield. This is not a constant $q_P/\mu$ relationship. Production in nongrowing cells is not established by this formulation.

\newpage
\section{Material balances and numerical method}
Let $B=XV$, $M=SV$ and $A=1000PV$ denote total biomass in g, methanol in g and product activity in U. The factor 1000 converts $P$ from U mL$^{-1}$ to U L$^{-1}$. The four states are $V,B,M,A$. Methanol feed $F$ is in g h$^{-1}$.
\begin{align}
\frac{dV}{dt}&=aF,& a&=\frac{1}{792}+\frac{0.12}{200}=0.0018626\unit{L\,g^{-1}},\\
\frac{dB}{dt}&=\mu B,&\frac{dM}{dt}&=F-q_SB,\\
\frac{dA}{dt}&=q_PB,& X&=B/V,\quad S=M/V.
\end{align}
The additive liquid volume approximation uses a 20\,$^{\circ}$C methanol feed density of 792 g L$^{-1}$ \cite{MethanolDensity} and the linked nitrogen stock \cite{PonteThesis}. Sampling, evaporation, base and antifoam additions are omitted. A 5\% working volume allowance is checked separately. Amount balances avoid ambiguity from changing volume, consistent with the course fed batch treatment \cite{CourseFedBatch}.

\begin{center}\tighttable
\captionof{table}{Feeding policies implemented in this study.}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{0.08\textwidth}Y@{}}\toprule
Case & Definition\\\midrule
CF & $F=14$ g h$^{-1}$.\\
CM & $F=q_S(0.03)B_0\exp(0.03t)$. A prescribed feed, not biomass feedback.\\
LM & $F=q_S(\mu_T)B_0\exp(\int_0^t\mu_T\,dt)$, with $\mu_T=0.01+0.0005t$ up to 60 h, then 0.04 h$^{-1}$.\\
CS & $F=q_SB/(1-aS)$, the ideal balance required for constant $S$.\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
CF, CM and CS target values follow the published strategy definitions. The 60 h LM ramp is an explicit study assumption. CM and LM targets need not equal the actual transient growth rate. Their feed histories are identical across the two kinetic models. CS uses each model's uptake rate, so its feed history differs between models. It represents an ideal setpoint policy, rather than a simulated physical controller.

Classical fourth order Runge Kutta uses a 0.01 h step. Integration stops at the first biomass, volume, methanol or 120 h limit. Event times are refined within the final step. All eight cases pass step halving, with maximum relative harvest activity change below $3\times10^{-10}$. Integrated substrate and activity balances are also checked. An independent closed form CS solution verifies the selected case to below $10^{-12}$ relative error. These are numerical checks, not experimental validation.

\section{Oxygen transfer and reactor design}
Derived demand is compared with gas transfer capacity at a fixed dissolved oxygen target \cite{PonteThesis,CourseFedBatch}.
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{OUR}_{V}=q_{O_2}X,\qquad
\mathrm{OTR}_{V}=k_La\,\Delta C_{\mathrm{lm}}.
\end{equation}
The adopted air saturation value at 30\,$^{\circ}$C is $C^*_{\mathrm{air}}=2.3\times10^{-4}$ mol L$^{-1}$ \cite{PonteThesis}. The 25\% air calibrated DO target gives $C_L=5.75\times10^{-5}$ mol L$^{-1}$. Oxygen enrichment changes $C^*$, while this absolute DO target is retained.

Gas flow is specified at 30\,$^{\circ}$C and 1 bar absolute. One mol CO$_2$ is assumed to replace each mol O$_2$ consumed, keeping dry gas flow constant. Let $g=G/V$, $c=C^*_{\mathrm{air}}/0.21$ and $y$ denote oxygen mole fraction. For plug flow gas and well mixed liquid,
\begin{align}
G&=\frac{60\,q_gV\,p}{RT},&y_{\mathrm{out}}&=y_{\mathrm{in}}-\frac{\mathrm{OUR}}{G},\\
\mathrm{OTR}_{V,\max}&=\frac{g}{c}(cy_{\mathrm{in}}-C_L)\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{k_Lac}{g}\right)\right].&&
\end{align}
Equation 9 integrates the gas balance and includes oxygen depletion at the capacity itself. At fixed vvm, gas blend, DO and $k_La$, volumetric capacity is constant. Pressure gradients and broth solubility corrections are omitted.

The transfer correlations use SI units, with $P_g/V$ in W m$^{-3}$, $u_s$ in m s$^{-1}$ and $k_La$ in s$^{-1}$ \cite{VanRiet,Yawalkar,Labik}.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
k_La_{\mathrm{water}}&=0.026(P_g/V)^{0.4}u_s^{0.5},\\
k_La_{\mathrm{electrolyte}}&=0.002(P_g/V)^{0.7}u_s^{0.2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Power follows $P_0=n_iN_P\rho N^3D_i^5$, with $N=1000/60$ s$^{-1}$ and $\rho=1000$ kg m$^{-3}$. Correlations give \WaterKla{} and \ElectrolyteKla{} h$^{-1}$ for water and electrolyte \cite{VanRiet,Yawalkar,Labik}. These are screening extrapolations. The 1000 h$^{-1}$ target requires broth measurement.

\section{Oxygen feasibility and feeding selection}

\fig{oxygen_design}{0.99}{Transfer screen for the selected inhibition case at \Setpoint{} g L$^{-1}$. Capacity uses the 1000 h$^{-1}$ qualification target and the stated gas balance.}

At harvest, $\mathrm{OUR}_V=\PeakVolOUR$ mmol L$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$ and total OUR is \PeakOUR{} mol h$^{-1}$. At 75\% inlet O$_2$ and 2 vvm, $H=(g/c)(cy_{\mathrm{in}}-C_L)=3.3207$ mol L$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$. Inverting Equation 9 gives
\begin{equation*}
k_La_{\mathrm{req}}=-\frac{4.7608}{0.00109524}\ln\left[1-\frac{1.20(0.52204)}{3.3207}\right]\approx\KlaRequired\unit{h^{-1}}.
\end{equation*}
At the 1000 h$^{-1}$ qualification target, capacity is \TransferCapacity{} mmol L$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$ and exceeds demand by at least 20\%. Air alone fails this screen. The DO loop adjusts agitation and enrichment.

Under inhibition, the best feasible grid point is \Setpoint{} g L$^{-1}$, giving \FinalActivity{} U mL$^{-1}$ and \FinalProductivity{} U L$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$. Productivity is \ProductivityGain\% above the 2 g L$^{-1}$ reference. Setpoints from 3.2 to 3.6 g L$^{-1}$ differ by less than 0.3\%. \section{Sensitivity, monitoring and control}
\fig{sensitivity}{0.98}{One input at a time sensitivity around the selected inhibition case. Each listed coefficient is changed by 20\%, while the setpoint and remaining inputs are held fixed.}

The scenarios are not confidence intervals. Large product curve coefficient uncertainty limits setpoint precision \cite{PonteThesis}. At constant substrate, faster growth shortens induction but also reduces accumulated activity. These effects largely cancel in $Q_P$, explaining its weak growth sensitivity. Product rate uncertainty directly changes activity and cost. Increasing the growth coefficient by 20\% violates the oxygen allowance despite little change in $Q_P$.

The methanol controller implementation is not modelled. An operating system would require sensor calibration, feed pump limits and tested tuning. High agitation is supported by the published stirred process, but strain specific shear limits and broth rheology remain unmeasured. Cell viability, foam and protease release should be checked when increasing agitation. The transfer correlations assume a low viscosity liquid and do not resolve these effects.

For example, $\Delta B=55(\FinalVolume)-27(2)\approx127.56$ g and methanol consumption is \MethanolConsumed{} g. Thus $Y_{X/S}=127.56/\MethanolConsumed\approx\BiomassYield$ g g$^{-1}$. \subsection{Industrial scale up}
Industrial transfer requires matching oxygen supply, mixing and heat removal, rather than holding rpm or vvm unchanged. Ponte reports poorer ROL performance after transfer to 50 L despite nominal DO control, showing that a probe reading alone does not establish equivalent cell conditions. A separate \pp{} study demonstrated 1000 L operation using pressure and a revised methanol feed, but produced a different intracellular enzyme. It supports pressure and feeding as scale up options, not transfer of its titer to ROL \cite{PonteThesis,ScaleUp}. A pilot study should first establish broth $k_La$, mixing time, off gas depletion, cooling duty and activity recovery. No industrial production rate is extrapolated from the present bench model.

\subsection{Conclusion and limits}
The inhibition model supports a candidate constant substrate operating region near the product rate peak. Its best screened point improves nominal productivity while requiring enriched oxygen and verified transfer capacity. The Monod comparator favours a different substrate policy. The decision is conditional on the kinetic inputs and the stated equipment qualification. The model excludes adaptation lag, oxygen limited physiology, degradation and spatial gradients. Its four reactor states do not make it an intracellular structured model \cite{CourseStructured}. Experimental kinetic checks and broth transfer measurements are required before operation.
